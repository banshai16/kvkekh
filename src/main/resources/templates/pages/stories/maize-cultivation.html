Maize is the second most important cereal in Meghalaya, with an average yield of 1.5 tonnes per hectare in 2010-11. It is used as human food (28 per cent), animal feed (11 per cent), poultry feed (48 per cent) and industrial products (12 per cent), and is a good source of carbohydrates, protein, fats and important vitamins and minerals. However, the local variety grown in Dangar, a small village on the Indo-Bangladesh border in Mawsynram C&RD Block, gave production and yield well below the national average, with poor-quality protein — a major concern for the KVK.

The KVK introduced Hybrid Quality Protein Maize (HQPM-1) through on-farm trials with a number of progressive farmers in the village. The objectives were to increase yield and make quality protein available to farmers; as a crop for both kharif and rabi seasons, it could also provide year-round income, and good quality protein when used as livestock feed. Trainings, method demonstrations and film shows were conducted to motivate farmers to adopt the new technology.

Results

Average yield
5.792 tonnes per hectare — almost four times that of the local variety

The stover provided feed for livestock and material for compost for the next crop. Considering the benefits and the farmers' acceptance of the technology, the KVK decided to conduct demonstrations the following year to popularise the variety.
