Intercropping maize with legumes, especially soybean, is traditionally practised in East Khasi Hills. However, intercropping French bean in maize as an additional crop had not been popularised, and farmers' intercropping practices were not scientific, so yields were low. KVK East Khasi Hills selected Smti. Shopsimai, a progressive farmer in Laitjem village, to introduce French bean intercropping in maize with a proper package of practices. French bean fetches a high market price all year round, adds to farm income, and improves the physical and chemical condition of the soil.

In 2012 the farmer was provided with the Vijay Composite variety of maize and the Selection 9 variety of French bean. Trainings were conducted and standard practices were demonstrated in the field: maize was sown in lines with regular row-to-row and plant-to-plant spacing, in a 1:1 ratio of maize to French bean. The importance of nutrient management was emphasised and recommended practices were encouraged.

Harvesting two crops from the same area at different times of the year consolidated the farmer's income, with French bean providing a good additional income alongside the main crop. This motivated her to continue the practice in the following years, and other farmers in the vicinity are willing to follow it.
